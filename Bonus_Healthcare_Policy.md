# Bonus Task: Policy Proposal for Ethical AI in Healthcare

## Title: Guidelines for Trustworthy AI in Clinical Settings

### 1. Patient Consent Protocols
*   **Informed Consent:** Patients must be explicitly informed if an AI system is being used to aid in their diagnosis or treatment plan.
*   **Opt-Out Mechanism:** Patients should have the right to request a human-only review of their case without penalty or degradation in care quality.
*   **Data Usage:** Consent forms must clearly state how patient data will be used for model training and whether it will be shared with third-party vendors.

### 2. Bias Mitigation Strategies
*   **Representative Training Data:** AI models must be trained on datasets that reflect the diversity of the patient population (age, gender, race, socioeconomic status) to prevent health disparities.
*   **Regular Audits:** Hospitals must conduct quarterly fairness audits using metrics like *Equalized Odds* to ensure the model performs equally well across all demographic groups.
*   **Feedback Loops:** A mechanism must exist for clinicians to report potential biases or errors they observe in clinical practice, which feeds back into model retraining.

### 3. Transparency & Explainability Requirements
*   **Clinical Decision Support (CDS):** AI recommendations must be accompanied by an explanation (e.g., "Sepsis alert triggered due to rising heart rate and low blood pressure"). "Black box" scores are not acceptable for critical care.
*   **Confidence Intervals:** Predictions should include a confidence score or uncertainty interval to help doctors weigh the risk.
*   **Accountability:** The final decision always rests with the human clinician. The AI is a tool, not a replacement for professional medical judgment.
